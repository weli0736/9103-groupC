# 9103 Group C — Awakening Heaven
## 1. Inspiration and Core Concept
> "The true death of an artwork is being forgotten. It only lives when continuously observed and appreciated."

Our project draws inspiration from ancient Chinese mythology and the aesthetic of the Mawangdui Silk Banner. We explore the philosophical relationship between an artwork and its audience. Through coding, we express this theme via a continuous interactive cycle:

The experience follows 5 connected states:

**Slumber → Awakening → Observation → Resonance → Oblivion **

- **Stage 1: The Slumber:** When there is no interaction, the artwork is forgotten. Heavy fog, generated by Perlin noise, obscures the painting. A massive time-based Sun and Moon cycle passes relentlessly above the fog. The lantern bearer (controlled by user input), representing the audience, remains in the dark.
- **Stage 2: The Awakening:** As the user moves the mouse, the lantern lights up with a warm glow. The light clears the fog, and the celestial cycle above smoothly fades away, signifying that the audience has arrived and started to observe the artwork.
- **Stage 3: Observation:** The hidden world underneath is revealed. Driven by the timebase module, stylized waves flow continuously. The Moon Tree and Sun Tree (Fusang) take turns pulsing with light, synchronized with the breathing halo of the central bronze bell, representing the internal life of the painting.
- **Stage 4: Resonance:** Clicking the bell triggers the audio module. The central dragons regain their true colors and react dynamically—rotating and breathing according to the sound amplitude. This illustrates the emotional resonance between the viewer and the art.
- **Stage 5: Oblivion:** If the user stops interacting for more than 10 seconds, the audience effectively "leaves". The lantern dims, the dragons turn white and freeze, and the fog slowly swallows the painting again. The giant Sun and Moon cycle reappears. The artwork returns to slumber, waiting to be remembered once more.

## 2. Techniques
The project uses p5.js. `sketch.js` coordinates the main scene. Each mechanic responds to shared interaction and idle states.

### Time-based Animation & Layer Dispatcher — Haocheng Gong
- Use `millis()` in `sketch.js` to manage the global `lastActiveTime` and `isIdle` state, coordinating all other members' modules centrally.
- Structure the scene with a dual-timebase architecture to seamlessly sandwich the fog layer: `timebase-2.js` (below the fog) and `timebase-1.js` (above the fog).
- **Above Fog (`timebase-1.js`):** Implement a 20-second Sun and Moon cycle. Use `Math.min()` to calculate a dynamic safe-rendering radius, ensuring the giant celestial bodies always stay within the canvas boundaries. Use `sin()` combined with `scale()` to create a heartbeat-like breathing animation. Read the fog's `reveal` value to dynamically map the opacity, allowing the celestial bodies to fade out smoothly as the fog clears.
- **Below Fog (`timebase-2.js`):** Use `sin()`, `cos()`, and `beginShape()` to generate a 10-second continuous flowing wave loop with traditional fine-line textures. Use `pow()` to smoothly attenuate the alpha values for the glowing tree fruits and the bronze bell's breathing halo.
- Optimize rendering performance by replacing excessive `drawingContext.shadowBlur` with lightweight, layered semi-transparent geometric fills, ensuring a stable 60 FPS experience.

**Key decision:** Splitting the time-based visuals into two separate files (`timebase-1` and `timebase-2`) allows the Perlin noise fog to perfectly sandwich the environment. Calculating the dynamic safe radius ensures the layout adapts flawlessly without clipping the celestial bodies.

### User Input — Junyu Cai
- Read mouse movement to activate the lantern and reset inactivity.
- Use `lerpColor()` to blend the lantern from cold blue to warm yellow, while expanding its glow.
- Use `mousePressed()` to cycle through three sounds: jade chime, war drum, and chant.

**Key decision:** Movement reveals the scene; clicks activate sound and dragon responses.

### Perlin Noise — Kristen Li
- Use Perlin noise and domain warping to create flowing fog.
- Use `createGraphics()` to separate fog and particle layers.
- Calculate fog on a low-resolution grid to reduce computation.
- Store local visibility in a reveal mask. Mouse movement gradually clears nearby fog; idle mode restores it.
- Use image brightness gradients to guide golden particles along cloud contours.
- Use `erase()` to fade old particle trails.

**Key decision:** Fog and cloud particles use separate drawing layers. A gradual reveal limit encourages slow exploration.

### Audio Feedback — Yuanze Lin
- Read sound amplitude to control the dragons’ response.
- Use `lerpColor()` to restore their colour.
- Use `lerp()` and `scale()` for smooth, amplitude-driven breathing.
- Use `translate()` and `rotate()` to turn each dragon around its centre.
- During idle mode, fade the audio and reset the dragons to grey and still.

**Key decision:** The dragons respond to user-triggered sounds rather than playing music automatically.
